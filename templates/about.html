About This Research

Research Question: "How do differences between original RAGTruth and RAGTruth++ span annotations affect the measured performance of a fixed hallucination detector on matched assistant responses?"

What is RAG?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that enhances language model responses by first retrieving relevant documents from an external knowledge base and then conditioning the model's output on both the original query and the retrieved context.

RAG systems aim to produce more factually accurate and grounded answers than purely parametric language models, but they are still susceptible to hallucination — generating content not supported by or contradicting the retrieved evidence.

What is Hallucination?

In the context of RAG systems, hallucination refers to a model generating statements, facts, or details that are not grounded in — or that directly contradict — the provided source documents.

Hallucinations are especially problematic in high-stakes domains such as medicine, law, and scientific research, where factual accuracy is essential. Detecting them reliably is an active area of ML research.

What is a Hallucination Span?

A hallucination span is a contiguous character-level region within an assistant response that a human annotator has identified as hallucinated. Spans are defined by a start and end character offset, a label (e.g., Conflicting, Unverifiable), and often a human-written reason explaining the annotation decision.

This application uses character offsets directly for all analysis — no tokenization is applied during span handling.

Why Use Annotations as Reference Labels?

Automated hallucination detectors must be evaluated against some ground truth. In the absence of an infallible oracle, human annotations serve as the reference standard.

Annotation quality directly determines what evaluation metrics mean. If the reference labels are incomplete or inconsistent, measured precision and recall reflect agreement with that specific annotation set — not an absolute truth about which text is hallucinated.

This application is careful to display these metrics as agreement measures, not factual verdicts.

RAGTruth vs RAGTruth++

RAGTruth is the original hallucination annotation dataset covering 18,000+ assistant responses generated by multiple LLMs, annotated with character-level span labels for hallucinated text regions.

RAGTruth++ is an expanded re-annotation of a subset of those responses. It may contain additional spans, revised span boundaries, updated labels, or newly added annotations that were absent from the original pass.

Important: Annotation disagreement between the two datasets does not automatically mean either dataset is incorrect. Hallucination annotation is a subjective and difficult task; reasonable annotators can disagree about span boundaries and labels while both applying valid judgment.

Why Evaluate Against Both Annotation Sets?

This research examines the fixed detector KRLabsOrg/lettucedect-base-modernbert-en-v1, which was run on 408 matched assistant responses and produced character-level hallucination span predictions.

By evaluating the same detector predictions against both annotation sets, the research measures how sensitive the evaluation metrics are to the choice of reference labels. A detector that appears to perform well against one annotation set may appear weaker against another — even when its predictions have not changed at all.

This illustrates why careful attention to annotation quality and version tracking is essential for fair and reproducible hallucination detection benchmarks.

This application processes all data locally. No data is transmitted to external services. All metrics are computed from the files you upload — no results are hardcoded.
